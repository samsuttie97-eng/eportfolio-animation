Digital Animation

Animated learning with Adobe Animate

Using Adobe Animate, I learned how animation can simplify complex ideas by combining movement, visuals, and narration to teach scientific topics effectively.

Educational Animation

I created a short animation in Adobe Animate that explains why chameleons change color, using simple animation techniques to present a scientific concept in a fun and engaging way. I started by researching the basic biology behind color change so I could explain it accurately but in a way that would make sense to a general audience. I then drafted a quick storyboard to plan how the animation would flow. This went from introducing the chameleon to showing how its skin adapts to different surroundings and protects itself from prey. In Adobe Animate, I used keyframes to control movement and transitions, giving the appearance that the chameleon is naturally shifting and changing its color. Motion tweens helped me animate the color transformations, letting the chameleon gradually blend into new environments instead of switching abruptly to a new color. I also used layered visuals to separate the background, chameleon body, bird, and text, which made the animation more organized and easier to edit. I then typed up a script for the narrator to say, to which I then input this text into an AI voice generator and layered it over the video in areas where narration was necessary. Overall, this project allowed me to combine creativity with information, and it helped me practice using Adobe Animate to tell a clear, scientific concept through motion, color, and layered design.
